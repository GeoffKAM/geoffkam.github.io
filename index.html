About

On the 2026–2027 academic job market

I am an economics Ph.D. candidate at Washington University in St. Louis, expecting to graduate in May 2027.

I am a macroeconomist who studies how venture capital is financed and how it shapes extreme outcomes, including top wealth inequality and innovation. My job market paper shows that, dollar for dollar, government spending that adds investors raises the number of venture capital-backed startups far more than spending on bigger deals, and that successful founders who become investors widen the gap.

Fields: Macroeconomics, Macro-Finance, Entrepreneurial Finance
